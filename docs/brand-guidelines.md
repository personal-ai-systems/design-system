# Personal AI Systems: working brand and customer experience

Revision: 6 November 2026. Working direction selected for implementation, not
an assertion of permanent brand approval. Applies across web properties; the
shared package remains independent of this product's marketing layout.

## Brand hierarchy

Personal AI Systems is the product brand. Creator Flow is the working product
name. CONSENS PTY LTD is the operator, not a competing headline or consultancy
pitch. Do not invent a logo family or pretend this is an established customer
platform. Use a consistent typographic wordmark until a logo is commissioned.

## Two audiences, one honest page

The paying customer is an independent travel/UGC creator, with small
hospitality teams as the adjacent segment. The immediate reader of the public
page, however, is a provider reviewing a grant application. Both read the same
source: there is no reviewer-only edition and no customer-only edition.

- Write for the creator first: outcome, workflow and limits in their terms.
- Answer the reviewer's questions explicitly, because they are factual claims
  about the same product: what it is, who it is for, what runs today, what is
  planned, who operates it and why a voice provider is part of it.
- Neither audience justifies invented traction, prices, partnerships or dates.
  A factual answer is the strongest one available; nothing on the page changes
  a provider's eligibility decision.
- Visual quality is reviewed separately from factual accuracy and grant
  compliance. Do not treat a visual pass as approval of a claim.

## Customer promise

**Turn the stories you capture into videos you feel confident sharing.**

For independent travel/UGC creators who have a story and media but get stuck
on narration, working in another language, appearing on camera or editing.
The product direction connects those steps; it is not another generic voice
generator. An accent is not a defect. Offer choices about language, voice and
delivery without making people feel inadequate.

Voice: specific, capable, warm, straightforward. Explain an outcome before a
technical feature. Avoid 'revolutionary', 'effortless', '10x', guaranteed income,
automatic accent removal, fabricated usage or claims of end-to-end availability.

## Four jobs to be done

| Customer situation | Barrier | Proposed outcome | Boundary |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A traveller has clips but avoids talking on camera | Exposure and production friction | A narrated, captioned travel story without appearing on screen | Not a claim of an available creator editor |
| A multilingual creator prefers telling the story in their first language | Translation and comfort with English narration | A reviewed English script and an approved narration voice | Translation/voice integration is planned; preserve meaning, not accent-shaming |
| A UGC creator or small hospitality operator has media for a brand brief | Brief, narrative and deliverables are disconnected | A focused stay/product story with requested formats | No promise of hotel partnerships, bookings, reach or revenue |
| A creator needs several social placements | Repeating layout and caption work | Portrait, feed and landscape exports from an approved story | Three aspect ratios are demonstrated; distinct short/long edits remain planned |

Initial focus is independent travel/UGC creators. Small hospitality teams are
an adjacent customer hypothesis, not a second full product launch.

## Visual direction

- Preserve the existing coastal photograph. It is illustrative generated art,
  not evidence of a real hotel, destination visit or customer partnership.
- White/light neutral page, near-black text, one primary action colour,
  restrained contrasting secondary accent. Photography supplies visual variety.
- Shared semantic roles, never colour-named public classes. Change values in
  core/theme sources, regenerate adapters; do not override disconnected copies.
- Use the mapped system sans family consistently for now. This is a deliberate
  working font choice: fast, licensed by the platform, readable, no font CDN.
  Proprietary reference-brand fonts and logos must not be copied.
- Marketing display scale may differ from application headings, but use named
  roles, rem units and deliberate breakpoints. Never viewport-scaled font sizes
  or negative tracking. All letter spacing is zero.
- Restrained 400/500/600 weights. Body at least 16px; secondary text remains
  legible. Text measure approximately 60-70 characters. Comfortable line height.
- Full-width section bands with constrained inner content. No nested cards,
  generic bento mosaic or carousel hiding the four primary use cases.
- 4/8px rhythm, generous section separation, consistent controls and alignment.
  Controls at least 44px, visible focus, reduced-motion support, native labels.
- Keep a hint of the following section visible below the hero. Do not crush
  copy into a fixed height on a short phone to force this.

## Page narrative

1. Product name, concrete product-category line, promise, target customer and
   genuine next action.
2. The customer problem, in their terms, not a list of implementation tools.
3. Four visible use cases: situation -> output -> benefit, no invented metrics.
4. Actual output demonstration, with origin and prototype scope stated nearby.
5. Three-step intended workflow: tell -> shape -> approve/export.
6. Voice in the workflow: the six real stages, each labelled working, planned or
   proposed; the provider named as an intended integration only.
7. Voice control and consent, accurate current/planned distinction.
8. Built and building: what runs today versus what is next, plus first-party
   public code as separate evidence, never as this product's source.
9. The product and how it is intended to earn: own product, paid access to the
   workflow, not agency production and not provider-credit resale.
10. One clear early-access enquiry route; no fake form success.

Keep the venture/grant rationale in a separate brief. A customer does not need
to scroll past TAM charts or the founder's grant application to understand the
product. Customer copy must sell the useful outcome, not credit eligibility.

## Footer contract

One compact footer, aligned to the page grid. Product/brand identity, operator
CONSENS PTY LTD, working contact, and only useful working links: How it works,
Contact and a factual Data & privacy section. No '#' destinations, imaginary
careers/blog/pricing/social accounts, partner badges, or placeholder policies.
The privacy wording must describe the actual implementation, not promise
certification or confidentiality that has not been established.

## Reviewer controls

Default page is customer-facing. No 'Concept Preview', Studio, Signal or
After Hours strip. Explicit `?review=1` enables colour-review controls, retains
the flag when switching variants and allows exporting proposed roles. Without
JavaScript those controls remain hidden. This is convenience, NOT access
control: static public source remains inspectable. Do not call it a private
admin panel. Colour proposals do not silently update canonical tokens.

## Truth and measurement

Current prototype evidence: local English transcription, captions/rendering,
three aspect-ratio exports using a common timeline. Planned: image selection,
Russian-to-English adaptation, integrated voice preparation, hosted creator
workflow and publishing connections. No customer numbers, timing reductions,
conversion gains, paid demand or testimonials are established.

A named voice provider may appear as an intended integration for the narration
stage. Distinguish implemented prototype adapters from an end-to-end, live-API
validated creator service; the former exist, the latter is not established.
Never imply partnership, an awarded grant,
'powered by' availability, a tested paid branch or accent removal. Speech
conversion preserves the delivery of a recording; it is not an accent fix.

Validate with creator interviews and task trials. Measure completion rate,
time from supplied material to approved export, correction rate, consent
comprehension and willingness to pay before adding numerical claims.
